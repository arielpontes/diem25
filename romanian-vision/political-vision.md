# We recognise that Romanians have given up on their political class — and they are right.

*Like every DiEM25 Romania vision document, this one is concrete about direction and deliberately silent on fine detail. Every policy we name already works somewhere in Europe, and we cite the laws so you can verify us. The fully specified, costed Romanian versions will be drafted democratically by our members — and, when the time comes, by candidates who put them before voters.*

For three decades the same parties have rotated through power, changing names, changing coalitions, changing nothing. Romanians have answered with some of the lowest trust in politicians and parliaments in Europe — and with emigration, the most honest vote of no confidence a people can cast. Into that vacuum of trust step the sellers of saviours and strongmen, promising to fix democracy by abolishing it.

We propose the opposite cure. The problem with Romanian democracy is not that ordinary people have too much power. It is that they have power for one day every four years, and none in between. We envision new layers of democracy between citizens and the politicians they no longer believe: institutions that put real budgets, real deliberation and real decisions in the hands of ordinary Romanians. Our Romania will be:

- **Democratic,** with political power exercised by citizens continuously, not surrendered at the ballot box every four years.
- **Decentralised,** pushing money and decisions down to towns, cities and neighbourhoods.
- **Transparent,** with every public leu traceable by any citizen.
- **Honest,** treating people as adults: saying what a policy costs, who pays and what remains undecided.

## What we propose

- **Participatory budgeting in every city.** A fixed share of local investment budgets decided directly by residents, who propose and vote on projects. Paris puts around 5% of its investment budget — on the order of €100 million a year — into the hands of its residents; Portugal ran the world's first national participatory budget in 2017. Romania is not starting from zero: Cluj-Napoca has run participatory budgeting for years. We propose making it the norm rather than the exception, with a legal framework guaranteeing that citizens' choices are binding.
- **Neighbourhood councils.** Standing councils at neighbourhood level with a consultative role in local decisions and a channel to bring residents' priorities into city hall, on the model of France's *conseils de quartier*, mandatory in larger French cities since the 2002 *loi relative à la démocratie de proximité*.
- **Citizens' assemblies.** For the questions career politicians are too compromised or too frightened to answer, randomly selected panels of ordinary citizens — a statistical portrait of the country — hear experts, deliberate and issue recommendations that parliament must formally answer. Ireland's Citizens' Assembly paved the way to the referendums on marriage equality and abortion; the German-speaking Community of Belgium made its citizens' council a permanent institution in 2019; France's Convention Citoyenne pour le Climat handed its president 149 climate proposals. Sortition is not a utopian gadget: it is working European constitutional practice.
- **Radical transparency.** Publication, in open and searchable form, of all public contracts, spending and subsidies; a public lobbying register; and genuine protection for whistleblowers, applying in earnest the EU Whistleblower Directive (2019/1937) that Romania transposed on paper.

We are sincere about what this document is: a direction, not a statute book. The thresholds, budgets and procedures must be set by Romanians deliberating — which is exactly the kind of politics this vision exists to create.
