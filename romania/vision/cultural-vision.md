# We recognise that Romania's greatest export has been its people — and we want a country that finally deserves them.

*Like every DiEM25 Romania vision document, this one is concrete about direction and deliberately silent on fine detail. Every policy we name already works somewhere in Europe, and we cite the laws so you can verify us. The fully specified, costed Romanian versions will be drafted democratically by our members — and, when the time comes, by candidates who put them before voters.*

Millions of Romanians build Germany's houses, harvest Italy's fields, care for Austria's elderly and staff the hospitals of France and Britain. Western Europe runs, in no small part, on Romanian work — and Romanians abroad have earned a respect as workers that they are too rarely granted as a nation, including by themselves. We reject the national inferiority complex. The problem was never the quality of Romanians; it was the quality of the institutions that kept failing them until they left.

Culture is how a people tells itself who it is. For too long, Romanians have been told a story of their own inadequacy — and the far right now offers the mirror-image fairy tale of mystical national greatness. We propose a third story, and it happens to be true: a confident, plural Romania, proud of its heritage and its diaspora alike, open to the world because it no longer fears it. Following DiEM25's cultural vision and [mέta, the Centre for Post-capitalist Civilisation](https://metacpc.org/), we see culture as a participatory instrument of social transformation, not a luxury for the few. Our Romania will be:

- **Confident,** telling the true story of a people who kept half of Europe running, instead of a story of shame or a compensatory myth of grandeur.
- **Pluralist,** treating its Hungarian, Roma, German and every other community as co-authors of Romanian culture, not guests in it — and ending, in practice and not just in law, the discrimination that Roma citizens face daily.
- **Internationalist,** in active solidarity with working people everywhere, starting with our own diaspora and the migrant workers who now come to Romania and deserve from us what we demand for Romanians abroad.
- **Alive,** where culture is something people do — in villages, neighbourhood houses of culture, schools and independent spaces — not only something they consume in a few big-city institutions.

## What we propose

- **Social security for artists.** Artists and cultural workers live from project to project, invisible to a social system built for permanent contracts. Germany has insured freelance artists since 1983 through the Künstlersozialkasse, splitting contributions between artists, the state and those who commission them; France's *intermittents du spectacle* regime adapts unemployment insurance to the rhythm of artistic work. Romania should guarantee its artists the same dignity.
- **A culture pass for the young.** Every Romanian turning eighteen should receive a state-funded budget to spend on books, concerts, cinema, museums and theatre, on the model of France's Pass Culture and the Italian bonus introduced with 18app.
- **Museums that belong to everyone.** Free admission to public museums at least one Sunday a month, as Italy has done nationally since 2014 with *Domenica al Museo* — and a funding formula that keeps county museums, libraries and houses of culture alive outside Bucharest and Cluj.
- **A cultural bridge to the diaspora.** Public support for Romanian-language culture, schools and media where Romanians actually live — Italy, Spain, Germany, the UK — treating the diaspora as part of the nation's cultural life, not as an ATM for remittances and a queue outside a consulate.

A country's self-esteem is not restored by monuments and anthems. It is restored when its people can live, create and be cared for at home as well as they are needed abroad.
