# The DiEM25 vision for Romania

*Participatory democracy. Tax justice. Sustainable development. Peace through diplomacy.*

Thirty-five years ago, Romanians were promised that the market and the West would bring them the living standards of the West. Much of what arrived instead was extraction: profits made from Romanian work leaving the country barely taxed, forests leaving as raw logs, and people leaving most of all. Our answer is not to turn against Europe — it is to demand for Romanians what the workers of Western Europe have already won for themselves. Everything we stand for below already exists, works, and is popular somewhere in the European Union. We simply refuse to accept that it stops at our border.

This is the direction DiEM25 organizes around in Romania: the values that guide us, and the European policies that inspire us because they have already proved themselves wherever people fought for them. Our members are now doing the work of turning this direction into concrete proposals for Romania — democratically, and grounded in research. Here is where we start.

## Participatory democracy: power between elections, not just at them

For three decades the same political class has rotated through power, and Romanians have answered with some of Europe's lowest trust in parties and parliaments — and with emigration, the most honest vote of no confidence a people can cast. We do not believe the cure is a saviour or a strongman. The problem is not that ordinary people have too much power; it is that they have power for one day every four years, and none in between.

We are inspired by the European institutions that put real budgets and real decisions in citizens' hands continuously: participatory budgeting, which lets residents directly decide part of their city's investment — practised from [Paris](https://www.theguardian.com/cities/2014/oct/08/parisians-have-say-city-first-20m-participatory-budget) to [Portugal](https://govinsider.asia/intl-en/article/how-portugal-launched-the-worlds-first-national-participatory-budget), and already pioneered in Romania by [Cluj-Napoca](https://stirileprotv.ro/stiri/social/bugetul-participativ-cluj-este-primul-orac-cu-proiecte-de-investic-ii-alese-de-comunitate.html); citizens' assemblies of randomly selected ordinary people, which in [Ireland](https://electoral-reform.org.uk/the-irish-abortion-referendum-how-a-citizens-assembly-helped-to-break-years-of-political-deadlock/), [Belgium](https://www.prospectmagazine.co.uk/magazine/belgiums-experiment-in-a-new-kind-of-democracy) and [France](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2020/nov/20/it-gave-me-hope-in-democracy-how-french-citizens-are-embracing-people-power) have answered questions career politicians were too compromised to touch; and radical transparency, where every public contract and every public leu is open and searchable by any citizen.

## Tax justice: an economy for the many who create its wealth

Romania taxes salaries, consumption and the neighbourhood shop hard, while multinationals move their Romanian profits abroad and great fortunes go almost untaxed — in the member state with the EU's highest in-work poverty. Our quarrel is not with the peoples of Western Europe, and it is emphatically not with the Romanian who opens a workshop, a farm, a shop or a software firm. That entrepreneur creates wealth that stays in the country and deserves the lightest, simplest state in Europe. Our quarrel is with corporate extraction.

We are inspired by the economies that reward building and tax extraction: by the EU's own [minimum tax on multinational profits](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_23_6712) and its [windfall levies on crisis profiteers](https://www.euronews.com/green/2022/09/15/eu-reveals-plan-to-pass-profits-from-fossil-fuels-on-to-those-who-need-it-most); by Estonia, where [a company is founded online in a day](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/start-a-company/) and reinvested profits are not taxed at all; by Spain's [solidarity tax on large fortunes](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2022/9/29/spain-agrees-on-new-asset-tax-for-wealthy-residents); by Germany, where workers have [elected representatives to company boards](https://www.bruegel.org/blog-post/codetermination-germany-role-model-uk-and-us) for half a century; and by [France](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/actualites/A18893?lang=en) and [Italy](https://ica.coop/en/media/news/marcora-law-supporting-worker-buyouts-thirty-years), where the law helps workers buy their own companies rather than watch them close. At the European level, we campaign within DiEM25 for [taxing the ultra-rich across borders](https://www.france24.com/en/france/20250913-zucman-tax-push-to-tax-the-super-rich-could-make-or-break-france-s-next-government) and for [a basic dividend paid from the returns on Europe's collective wealth](https://diem25.org/what-comes-after-capitalism/).

## Sustainable development: our nature is not for sale

Remember Roșia Montană: a foreign corporation tried to blast open a mountain with cyanide, the Romanian people stopped it in the streets — and the corporation sued Romania before a private tribunal, and lost. The pattern repeats across the Carpathians, whose old-growth forests feed foreign-owned sawmills, and under the Black Sea, whose gas is developed from Vienna: our resources, their profits, our pollution. Meanwhile Romania suffers some of the worst energy poverty in the EU.

We stand with DiEM25's [Green New Deal for Europe](https://gnde.diem25.org): the ecological transition is not a sacrifice to be endured but the greatest opportunity in a generation — if its costs fall on those who profited from destruction, not on the weakest. We are inspired by Slovenia, which [wrote the right to drinking water into its constitution](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2016/nov/18/slovenia-adds-water-to-constitution-as-fundamental-right-for-all) and shielded water from privatisation; by Denmark, which built one of the world's great wind industries by [requiring new projects to offer ownership stakes to local residents](https://www.wri.org/update/sustained-portfolio-policies-have-transformed-denmarks-power-sector); and by the EU's own [rules against illegal timber and deforestation](https://www.wri.org/insights/explain-eu-deforestation-regulation), which need enforcement in Romania, not more paper.

## Peace through diplomacy: security that starts with resilience

Romanians' fear of Russia is not paranoia — it is memory — and we stand with the people of Ukraine as with every people suffering invasion. But fear is a poor procurement officer. A country that spends billions on fighter jets while its hospitals, schools and civil protection crumble, and whose people keep emigrating, is not becoming more secure. A depopulated, distrustful country is insecure no matter how advanced its weapons.

We believe Romania, as a direct neighbour of the war, should be one of Europe's loudest voices for negotiations, ceasefire and a comprehensive peace with real security guarantees. We are inspired by [Europe's neutral democracies](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/europe/2026/01/02/eus-neutral-member-states-face-hard-choices-as-war-returns-to-continent/) — Austria, Ireland, Malta — which are safe, prosperous and attacked by no one, proof that security in Europe is possible without a blank cheque for the arms industry; we are honest that Romania's geography and history are its own, and that any question of alliances belongs to the Romanian people, decided democratically. What we demand already: full parliamentary and public scrutiny of every major arms purchase, and treating hospitals, energy independence, cybersecurity and social cohesion as the core of national security — because they are.

This vision is also an invitation. The research, the local knowledge and the democratic drafting of concrete proposals come next, and they are open to everyone who wants Romania to work for the many. Join us in writing them.
