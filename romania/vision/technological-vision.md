# Romanian engineers power the world's software. We want their talent to power Romania.

*Like every DiEM25 Romania vision document, this one is concrete about direction and deliberately silent on fine detail. Every policy we name already works somewhere in Europe, and we cite the laws so you can verify us. The fully specified, costed Romanian versions will be drafted democratically by our members — and, when the time comes, by candidates who put them before voters.*

Romania has some of the fastest internet in Europe and exports software talent to every tech hub in the world — yet its citizens still shuttle paper folders between counters, and its couriers and drivers are managed by algorithms owned abroad, with none of the rights of employees and none of the freedoms of the self-employed. We build the digital future for everyone except ourselves.

With DiEM25, we envision a **digital commons**: technology owned and governed by the people who use it, not by platform oligarchs who operate like feudal lords. Our Romania will be:

- **Technologically sovereign,** running its public digital infrastructure on software it controls, not on licences and clouds it rents from Big Tech.
- **Creative,** turning its extraordinary technical talent toward public problems — not only toward outsourcing contracts for others' products.
- **Responsible,** putting technology at the service of people and solidarity, not surveillance and profit.
- **Humane,** supporting a Universal Declaration of Human Rights for the digital age that protects people's ownership of their data and bans autonomous AI-based weapons.

## What we propose

- **Public money, public code.** Software developed with public funds should be released as open source, and public administration should prefer open-source solutions — as Italy's Digital Administration Code (Codice dell'Amministrazione Digitale, art. 68–69) already requires and as France's 2016 Loi pour une République numérique mandates for public data, open by default.
- **A state that stops asking for the dosar cu șină.** Digital public services built on the *once-only* principle: the state never asks a citizen for a document another public institution already holds. Estonia has run its e-government this way for two decades on X-Road — open-source infrastructure that other countries, including Finland, have simply adopted. So can Romania.
- **Digital tools for democracy.** Run the participatory budgeting, neighbourhood consultations and citizens' assemblies of our political vision on free software such as Decidim, the open-source participation platform built by Barcelona and now used by cities across Europe — so that the infrastructure of Romanian democracy cannot be owned by anyone.
- **Rights for algorithmically managed workers.** A presumption of employment for platform workers and transparency of the algorithms that direct them, on the model of Spain's *Ley Rider* (Real Decreto-ley 9/2021) and the EU Platform Work Directive (2024/2831), implemented ambitiously rather than minimally. The courier is a worker; the app is a boss; the law should say so.
- **Data dignity.** Real enforcement of the GDPR against large platforms operating in Romania, and support at EU level for socialising the data wealth that platforms extract from all of us — so that 'free' services stop turning citizens into the product.

Romania's engineers have proven what Romanians can build when institutions get out of the way. We want institutions that finally build with them.
