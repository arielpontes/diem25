## 2. Escape from Debt-Serfdom: Economic, institutional and social reforms

Never in History has a bankruptcy been overcome either with new borrowed
money or with the extension of older loans. To extricate the country from
Debt-Serfdom, the precondition is the radical restructuring of private and
public debts. In addition, deep reforms are required which:

- protect waged labor from the merciless class war unleashed against it by
  the oligarchy, with the troika as its great helper

- strengthen creative entrepreneurship against the predatory rent-seeking of
  the oligarchic establishment

- exploit public property with the public interest as the guiding principle

- transform the predator-state into a helper-state

- protect the have-nots while indirectly boosting economic activity

Specifically, MeRA25 proposes the immediate legislation of the following
SEVEN REFORMS, which are presented here in full detail:

### **2.1 SEVEN ECONOMIC & INSTITUTIONAL REFORMS**

**REFORM 1 - Primary surpluses that abolish austerity**

The country today is bound to permanent austerity until 2060 - specifically,
to a primary surplus target of 3.5% of GDP from 2018 through 2022 and then
2.2% from 2023 through 2060.

The IMF itself (a) concluded that achieving this target will prevent the
very recovery its achievement requires, and (b) calculated the probability
of achieving this target at below 4%. In plain words, the country has
committed itself to a long-term target of bleeding society dry that neither
should nor can be achieved.

- MeRA25 will legislate a new maximum primary surplus target of 1.5% of GDP
  and a minimum of 0% of GDP depending on the growth rate of nominal GDP,¹
  to be deposited in a special account from which the creditors will be
  repaid.

> ¹ Specifically, the primary surplus target will be 1.5% of nominal GDP as
> long as the latter grows by at least 3% annually, will fall to 1% while
> the nominal GDP growth rate ranges between 1% and 3%, and will drop to
> zero when the nominal GDP growth rate falls below 1%.

**REFORM 2 - Public Debt Restructuring**

Unsustainable public debt is the driving force of our Debt-Serfdom. There
is, therefore, no solution other than the substantive restructuring of the
public debt.

MeRA25 will institute (through the exchange of bonds/IOUs²):

- annual debt repayments to the troika (ESM-IMF-ECB) proportional to the
  growth rate of nominal GDP and the legislated primary surplus (see Reform
  1), and

- the determination of the total amount of debt owed to the troika
  (principal and interest) proportional to the level of nominal GDP

In summary, the proposed debt swap prescribes a haircut inversely
proportional to the growth of economic activity and total incomes: rapid
growth will mean a zero haircut for the creditors. But the more anemic the
growth, the greater the haircut they will suffer. In this way, in essence,
the creditors are turned into partners in the country's recovery, since
they will be paid in full only if the country recovers significantly.

> ² Our current IOUs to the troika are exchanged for new bonds subject to
> the new calculation of repayments and obligations.

**REFORM 3 - Public Company for the Restructuring of Private Debts**

Restructuring the public debt is not enough in an economy where citizens owe
100 billion in "non-performing loans" to the banks, while the banks owe more
than 50 billion to the state (and private parties as a whole - households
and businesses - owe another 100 billion to the state). It is obvious that
no market solution exists to such a problem of generalized bankruptcy. The
troika's insistence on solving the problem with market solutions - that is,
selling the banks' "red" loans at knock-down prices to private parties
(mainly foreign), who then, through the threat of foreclosure auctions, will
recover a percentage of these loans' face value, keeping as profit the
difference between the latter and the price at which they bought the loans -
is not only morally and socially condemnable but foolish even by the "cold"
criteria of the market.

- MeRA25 will legislate the establishment of a Public Company for the
  Restructuring & Management of Private Debts (ΔΕΑΔΙΧ), which will have a
  dual aim: to protect the borrower and the primary residence, but also the
  banks, restoring their normal function as credit institutions.

  - The most "red" of the loans are gradually _transferred_ from the banks
    to ΔΕΑΔΙΧ. In exchange, the banks issue IOUs of equal face value
    guaranteed by the state, which the banks keep on their books as an
    asset, and which ΔΕΑΔΙΧ commits to repay over the long term from its
    future revenues.

- A moratorium is legislated on foreclosure auctions of (a) primary
  residences and (b) shops/commercial premises below a certain value

- The right is legislated for borrowers whose loans have already been sold
  to private parties to buy back their loans at the same price at which the
  private parties bought them

- ΔΕΑΔΙΧ commits to keeping the "red" loans on its books until the price of
  the encumbered properties exceeds a level close to the pre-crisis price.
  Until then, borrowers pay rent to ΔΕΑΔΙΧ (for the right to remain in
  their home), the level of which is determined by the local community
  (e.g. the Municipality) with regard to their incomes and local conditions.

- When the market prices of the properties exceed the amount of the loans,
  the debtors will have the right to buy back their old loan from ΔΕΑΔΙΧ
  either in installments or with a lump-sum payment reflecting, in the case
  of a primary residence or small-to-medium commercial property, the
  principal they owe (without interest and penalties).

With the establishment of ΔΕΑΔΙΧ the following are achieved simultaneously:
the protection of the primary residence and of small and medium owners from
the vulture funds and from despair, the prevention of a new slump in the
real estate market, the preservation of the banks' remaining capital and,
consequently, the return to normality in the provision of liquidity to the
economy by the banking system.

**REFORM 4 - Tax rates**

Debt-Serfdom is sustained by the absurdity of extremely high tax rates in an
economy where turnover (and therefore public revenue) is shrinking.

MeRA25 will legislate:

- Reduction of the maximum VAT rate from 24% to 15% and 18%, depending on
  whether payments are made electronically/by card (15%) or in cash (18%)

- A progressive business tax, reducing the tax on the (few) large
  enterprises from 29% to 26%, on medium enterprises to 20% and on small
  ones to 15%

- Abolition of the 100% advance payment of taxes

- Abolition of the solidarity tax for incomes below €30,000, retention of
  the tax-free threshold and reduction of the rates for incomes from
  €12,000 to €15,000. The tax reduction will increase consumers'
  disposable income, resulting in higher demand and containment of
  recessionary pressures, while the tax reduction for businesses will give
  them breathing room in liquidity and attract new investment.

- A new special levy on banks (modeled on the British bank levy) amounting
  annually to 0.2% of the banks' liabilities, which will exclusively
  strengthen the social security funds

- A change in the terms for granting the Golden Visa to foreign nationals
  from outside the EU (currently granted to them, under a law of the
  Samaras government, if they purchase real estate worth 250 thousand
  euros) so that

  - they are granted only in exchange for investments that create at least
    ten (10) new full-time jobs, and,

  - their income from properties sublet through websites such as Airbnb is
    taxed with a new special tax³

- Re-establishment of the incorruptible unit of SDOE (the Financial and
  Economic Crime Unit) and revival of the algorithmic auditing of tax
  evasion of 2015 which, had the capitulation to the troika and the
  oligarchy not occurred, would have exposed hundreds of thousands of tax
  evaders of the 2000-2014 era

The reduction of tax rates will increase consumers' disposable income and
weaken the incentive for tax evasion, resulting in increased demand, a
recovery of incomes and, ultimately, an <u>increase in public revenue.</u>

> ³ In 2014 the Samaras government inaugurated the Golden Visa program under
> which, e.g., a Russian or Chinese investor who desires a permanent
> residence permit in Greece and, consequently, a permit to stay anywhere in
> Europe, can obtain it by buying a property for 250 thousand euros. As they
> are not obliged to make personal use of the property, the new owners live
> in London or Berlin, renting out their Greek property exclusively to
> foreign visitors through, e.g., the Airbnb platform. In other words the
> Greek state provides incentives for the reduction of apartments available
> to permanent residents, with the largest share of the rents passing from
> one foreign account to another.

**REFORM 5 - Public Non-Bank Payments System**

In 2015 the troika closed the banks to blackmail the country into
capitulating to Debt-Serfdom. Beyond that, the bankrupt banks operate as a
cartel that charges exorbitant amounts for electronic transactions.

- MeRA25 will institute a Public Non-Bank Payments System (ΔΕΣΠ) which will
  allow natural and legal persons to transact with one another, and with
  the State, without the intermediation of the banking system. Each tax
  number (ΑΦΜ) will correspond to a TAXIS account which the citizen or
  business owner can credit and be credited with money electronically. The
  benefits of ΔΕΣΠ are mainly five:⁴

- Mutual cancellation of debts

- The ability of the state to borrow directly from taxpayers, in exchange
  for tax discounts⁵

- ΔΕΣΠ will constitute an alternative payments system, competing with the
  private banks, which will be forced to reduce their transaction fees

- The ability to create liquidity for the financing of programs, e.g., for
  combating poverty⁶

- Reduction of the threat of a Grexit by the creditors, since ΔΕΣΠ will
  significantly reduce the cost (and, therefore, the fear) of Grexit

> a</u> provision is legislated whereby €1,000 that the taxpayer transfers

**REFORM 6 - Conversion of TAIPED and the Superfund into a Development Bank**

The state today is committed to liquidating public property for crumbs that
are thrown into the black hole of the debt.

- MeRA25 will legislate:

  - Granting a banking license to TAIPED-Superfund (the privatization
    fund), converting it into the Development Bank the country needs

  - The new Development Bank keeps (whatever is left of) the public
    property, using it as collateral to raise resources to be directed
    toward financing (a) public investments that showcase (and increase)
    the public property itself - in cooperation, case by case, with private
    parties - and (b) private investments

  - All privatizations are frozen until, at a minimum, the prices at which
    certain assets would be sold recover. Public goods such as energy,
    water, beaches etc. are excluded from any sale regardless of prices and
    conditions

  - The shares of the new Development Bank are given to the social security
    funds, thus strengthening their capitalization

  - In the event that some public asset is sold in the future, the
    following terms apply:

    - a minimum level of investment by the buyer

    - collective agreements and guaranteed workers' rights

    - commitments benefiting local communities

    - retention of a share of the equity by the state, to be passed to the
      social security funds with the aim of permanently strengthening them.

**REFORM 7 - Respect for waged labor and creative entrepreneurship**

**Waged labor:** For years now in Greece, the working class's fundamental
achievement - the right of waged labor to be treated as something more than
a commodity or a service rendered - has been lost. Normal, dependent waged
labor is presented as the provision of services by an external contractor
working with the notorious "blokaki" (freelancer's invoice book). MeRA25
commits to the immediate abolition of this practice, which keeps hundreds of
thousands of workers in insecurity and at higher tax rates.

- MeRA25 will legislate:

  - the immediate and mandatory enrollment in IKA (the main social security
    fund) of all wage earners employed more than eight (8) hours per week

  - the legislation of a framework of flexible collective bargaining

  - the abolition of the recent provisions which, in essence, prohibit the
    majority of a union's members from deciding on strike action

**Creative entrepreneurship:** In a country where on average 75% of the
profits of small and medium enterprises end up as tax and social security
contributions to the state, recovery is a midsummer night's dream. Beyond
the reduction of tax rates and the abolition of the advance payment of taxes
(see Reform 4),

- MeRA25 will legislate:

  - a "ceiling" on the totality of businesses' social security and tax
    obligations at 50% of their profits

  - tax and social security immunity for five (5) years for newly founded
    small enterprises (start-ups).

### **2.2 ALWAYS WITH THE WEAK: Tackling the Humanitarian Crisis, Labor, Pensions**

**Humanitarian Crisis:** Every society is judged by how it treats its
weakest members. Over the last ten years the greatest cost of the crisis was
borne by the weakest shoulders. This national disgrace must end.

- MeRA25 will legislate the immediate support of the economically weakest
  families through the provision of a guaranteed minimum income (ΕΕΕ) to
  those officially below the poverty line as it was defined in 2010, so
  that no family need live below this threshold. At the same time, MeRA25
  commits to the full retention of the existing disability, child and other
  benefits.⁷

  - In a first phase, the ΕΕΕ will be paid by entering the required credits
    into the accounts of the families in question within the new ΔΕΣΠ
    system (see Reform 5 above). [To the extent that everyone, e.g. the
    supermarkets, has taxes to pay off, there is an incentive to accept
    electronic payments via ΔΕΣΠ.]

  - In a second phase, a percentage of the increase in public revenue will
    be used for the payment of the ΕΕΕ with the aim of gradually replacing
    up to 50% of the payments initially made via ΔΕΣΠ with transfers to the
    beneficiaries' bank accounts.

**Labor:** Harsh austerity cannot crush the people of waged labor without
first stripping them of every right. Necessary conditions for labor rights
to acquire substance and for wage earners to be protected from vulgar
exploitation are the recovery of economic activity (hence also the reduction
of tax rates - see Reform 4), the abolition of waged labor by "blokaki" (see
Reform 7), the guaranteed minimum income for all families (without any
reduction of the existing disability, child and other benefits - see above)
and, of course, the increase of the minimum wage. Without these, the
restoration of collective bargaining that the government preaches would
resemble the useless right of a poor man to buy... yachts and airplanes.

- MeRA25 will institute (a) the gradual increase of the minimum wage by 10%
  annually until it reaches 800 euros per month, and (b) flexible but
  strong collective bargaining using the model of German industry

> ⁷ It is noted that the troika's aim was, and remains, the complete
> replacement of all existing benefits by a guaranteed minimum income - with
> the goal of reducing total spending on benefit transfers.

**Pensions:** Beyond the guaranteed minimum income, which will essentially
restore EKAS (the pensioners' solidarity benefit) for everyone, the current
government's commitment to new pension cuts in 2019 will be immediately
revoked and, on the contrary, pensions will be strengthened by the new bank
levy (see Reform 4) while the capital of the social security funds will be
strengthened by the transfer to them of the shares of the new Development
Bank (see Reform 6).

## 3. Green Transition

The recent tragedy at Mati, together with the earlier one of the Mandra
floods, demonstrate the bitter truth that we are collectively responsible,
as Greek society, for our post-war violation of Nature and our failure to
live in harmony with it.

The post-war model of expansion (and in no sense development) was based on
the plundering of natural resources. Even today, when the crisis has
curtailed our assaults on Nature, the over-expansion of tourism threatens to
destroy all the country's assets that draw visitors to it. At the same time,
the crisis and the priorities of creditors and oligarchy push the country
toward ignoring our international duty to limit climate change.

Creditors, oligarchy and local "power brokers" demand daily the surrender of
the remaining natural wealth to private interests - something which not only
will not make us less poor but will make us poorer in the long run, since
the natural resources squandered cannot be replaced. **We will not allow
this, exactly as we will not allow the entrenchment of Debt-Serfdom, the
Desertification of Greece and the burial of Hope!**

- MeRA25 puts forward a **Green Transition Program** concerning energy,
  transport, industry/crafts, tourism, agricultural policy, waste
  management etc.

**3.1 ENERGY**: The potential for development through the extraction and
use of fossil fuels has been exhausted worldwide. Development in the future
will come from technologies that achieve a zero, or even negative,
greenhouse gas balance. MeRA25 commits to:

- A program of independence from fossil fuels by 2040

  - Expansion of Renewable Energy Sources (RES) balancing economies of
    scale with environmental protection of the landscape, natural
    ecosystems, and their natural beauty

  - Combined exploitation of local potential for geothermal, solar, hydro
    and biomass energy, as well as wind turbines for power generation

  - The producers of RES energy may belong to a wide spectrum of producers,
    ranging from PPC (the Public Power Corporation) to initiatives of
    "energy citizens," as in other European countries

- Emphasis on local energy production, on bioclimatic architecture, and on
  other innovations (e.g. LED technology in the lighting of public spaces)

**3.2 TRANSPORT**: An essential part of designing humane cities and of
contributing to the global campaign to limit climate change is the gradual
reduction of the kilometers traveled by privately owned conventional cars
and trucks, with a transition to electric mobility, bicycle lanes and
fixed-track transport.

- MeRA25 will set in motion the renegotiation of the privatization contract
  of OSE (the Greek railways) (with the aim of significantly increasing
  investment in modern electric trains), the obligation of oil companies to
  provide fast electric-vehicle charging points at fuel stations, a charge
  on ships docking in port proportional to the pollutants they emit, and
  the creation of bicycle lanes along all the main arteries of the cities.

**3.3 INDUSTRY-CRAFTS:** We support initiatives for the development of
crafts and small-scale production and entrepreneurship, with particular
emphasis on energy, water and raw materials that draw on the country's
history, traditions, culture and natural resources.

- MeRA25 legislates

  - tax breaks in favor of replacing dirty with clean forms of energy

  - taxation of the environmental cost of industries which could have been
    avoided through investment in green technologies

  - public investment in the cooperation of the Greek shipyards and PPC for
    the construction of wind turbines to be installed in remote sea areas
    (as happens, e.g., in the North Sea) as well as wave-power generation
    units (as, e.g., in Portugal).

**3.4 WASTE MANAGEMENT**: The future demands reduction, prevention, reuse
and sorting and recycling at the source.

- MeRA25 will introduce incentives and disincentives so that:

  - The percentage of waste going into the green (mixed) bin is below 10%,
    eliminating the need to find landfill sites

  - Bio-waste is put to use through production units for biofuels, compost
    and other value-added materials

  - Recycling is carried out compulsorily in all public organizations and
    gradually in private business premises.

**3.5 TOURISM**: Greece cannot offer large-scale tourism without destroying
that which draws foreign visitors to our country. Our cities and villages
must not be emptied of their inhabitants, with houses and apartments handed
over en masse to foreign visitors.

- MeRA25 will legislate:

  - The limitation of the size and number of tourist facilities

  - The drastic reduction of licenses for the de facto privatization of
    beaches (e.g. umbrellas, sunbeds) so that on every beach at least 80%
    is fully free of commercial activities

  - A maximum rental of private apartments and houses (e.g. through Airbnb)
    of 30 days during the November-June period and another 30 days during
    the July-October period (with a gap of at least 30 days between rentals
    falling within the two periods)

  - The imposition of a special tax on shipping companies, per mile
    traveled by their vessels in Greek seas during the previous year, which
    will exclusively fund the annual cleaning of the beaches of islands and
    islets, the seabed etc. of plastics, bags and other litter.

**3.6 AGRICULTURE-LIVESTOCK-FORESTRY-FISHERIES**: As with tourism, the
comparative advantage of Greek agriculture and livestock farming is its
small scale.

- MeRA25 will legislate guided by:

  - Food self-sufficiency in quality products produced while protecting the
    soils, biodiversity, the country's natural resources and cultural
    heritage

  - Low-impact fishing, with a limit on the number of fish farms and the
    imposition of strict rules regarding feed and stocking density for
    those that remain

  - The systematic reduction of pesticides and chemical soil enhancers, and
    the complete exclusion of genetically modified organisms (and of the
    chemical pesticides sold together with them) from the multinationals
    that develop them

  - Emphasis on organic production

  - The need for continuous clearing of forest areas (litter, flammable
    materials) by crews of local government and PPC (especially in areas
    near its installations and cables)

  - Water conservation through reuse

  - The utilization of residues, e.g. for bioenergy, production of
    fertilizers/compost

  - The restructuring of crops in a way that suits local conditions

**3.7 WATER RESOURCES:** Wherever in the world water and its management
were privatized, the result was catastrophic.

- MeRA25 proposes a constitutional provision making the state exclusively
  responsible for water and its management. In addition it legislates so
  that:

  - The current model of unlimited waste is abolished

  - The maintenance and improvement of the network becomes a priority, as
    does the reuse of water

  - Desalination is set in motion on those islands where local water
    resources are insufficient, using electricity produced exclusively from
    RES

  - Local, decentralized treatment units are activated.

**3.8 BUILT & NATURAL ENVIRONMENT:** MeRA25 supports the preservation of
the remaining open spaces, such as Elliniko, opposing the construction of
pharaonic projects.

- MeRA25 will proceed with:

  - The rescission of the contract ceding Elliniko, as a first token of its
    commitment to protecting citizens' rights against the voracious
    oligarchy

  - Assuming the political cost of clashing even with small and medium
    owners who, they too, demand the right to privatize public space
    (whether in the context of the "legalization of unauthorized buildings"
    or, e.g., in the conversion of our beaches into space for renting
    sunbeds/umbrellas)

  - Legislation so that areas like Mati are not rebuilt as they were, as a
    token that Greek society learns from its mistakes.

  - Completion of the land registry, land uses, forest maps, maritime
    spatial planning, foreshore delineation, and the ratification of the
    relevant European Treaties.

**3.9 ANIMAL RIGHTS:** A society that behaves barbarically toward animals
cannot boast of being humane.

- MeRA25 will legislate:

  - Prohibiting the keeping of dogs tethered, the confinement of animals or
    birds in suffocating spaces or conditions, as well as the feeding of
    animals destined for our table with feed that goes against their nature
    and their/our health

  - Strengthening, morally and materially, the animal welfare organizations
    and the volunteering that assist in the care of stray animals

  - Preventing the killing of living creatures for entertainment, and the
    gradual transition from hunting toward Greeks learning to visit Nature
    in order to observe and photograph animals in their natural environment
    - instead of shooting them

## 4. Education-Culture-Media

In the era of the collapse of Greek capitalism, public education and the
cultural activities which, before 2010, were funded by the state or by
state-fed oligarchs could not but suffer an enormous blow. At the same time,
in a country where culture and aesthetics are battered at every moment by
media outlets belonging to the oligarchs - whose survival depends on the
liquidity indirectly provided to them by the troika - it is natural that the
reproduction of Debt-Serfdom is interwoven with the degradation of our
cultural environment. On the other hand, the crisis brought the flowering of
new cultural, extra-systemic activities which give rise to the hope that,
just as the generation of the '30s "produced" culture out of crisis, so
will the generation of the '10s.

**4.1 EDUCATION:** MeRA25 considers that the degradation of education and
learning in "Debt-Serfdom Greece" is so deep, and its roots so strong, that
no government can bring about the educational revolution required. At least
eight pathologies demand a cross-party approach that transcends the partisan
confrontations inside and outside Parliament:

1. The atavistic attachment of pupils and students, and their families, to
   the "institution" of the frontistirio (private cram school), even when
   it is clear that it offers nothing

2. Society's addiction to the passive reproduction of information, which it
   even identifies with the "objective assessment" of our young people

3. Our society's relative indifference to the quality of the learning young
   people acquire from schools and universities, compared with the burning
   desire for (ultimately) devalued school-leaving certificates and degrees

4. A society that ignores the essential difference between information and
   skill, between schooling and learning, and between knowledge and wisdom

5. Left-wing student factions that insist on labor rights which it is
   impossible for universities to deliver within a capitalist system that
   systematically generates unemployment and underemployment (Note: if such
   a thing were possible, then why do they oppose capitalism?)

### **6.3 CONSTITUTIONAL REVISIONS**

The current Article 110 of the Constitution provides for a difficult
revision procedure in which citizens have no substantive role. Instead, the
revision of the Constitution is the exclusive privilege of politicians,
their constitutional scholars and the parliamentary parties, who discuss
and decide behind the citizens' backs.

In practice, under the current Article 110, a parliamentary majority is
required to decide whether the next Parliament, after the intervening
elections, will revise the Constitution, and in whatever direction that
Parliament decides. That is, a party can promise that if the people give it
a majority at the next elections it will change the Constitution in
direction A or B, without however any binding commitment to do so - and the
programmatic inconsistency of the parties is a given.

The Constitution must be the basic charter, the social and political
contract that determines how popular sovereignty is exercised and how a
society has decided to be governed. In the present Constitution of Greece
the people have no right to a say over its content, or over whether it
needs to change and in what direction.

- MeRA25 will legislate the designation of Article 110 of the Constitution
  **as subject to revision**, **_so that the next Parliament deletes
  paragraphs 5 and 6 and revises paragraphs 2, 3, and 4 of Article 110 as
  follows_**

  - **_Paragraph 2:_** The need to revise the Constitution is determined
    only by the citizens. The revision procedure (amendment, addition, or
    repeal of an article of the Constitution) begins only if any group of
    citizens submits a specific proposal to the President of the Republic
    or to Parliament, having collected at least 80,000 signatures in
    support of its proposal within a period of no more than 18 months

  - **_Paragraph 3:_** Their validity is confirmed and a referendum is
    called on a date no earlier than six months and no later than one year
    away. If the proposal is accepted by a simple majority of the citizens
    who took part in the referendum, it is added to the Constitution of
    Greece. The result of the referendum is binding and no minimum turnout
    is required for it to be considered valid

  - Every successful proposal for constitutional revision is published in
    the Government Gazette within ten days of the holding of the referendum
    and takes immediate effect upon publication.
